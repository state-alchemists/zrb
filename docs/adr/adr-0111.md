# ADR-0111 — On macOS, dictation captures through the system's voice processing to take zrb's voice out

**Status:** Accepted

**Context.** Barge-in (ADR-0076) lets the user talk over zrb, and ADR-0105's guards decide whether what was heard reaches the model. They cannot clean it: when the user really does talk over zrb on laptop speakers, the utterance is accepted, and its audio is the user's voice and zrb's together. The transcript mixes them. Measured on a MacBook's own speakers and microphone, with a spoken clip playing as zrb plays speech and the user talking over it, vosk wrote "…when it comes on friday it will be sunny in the morning with clouds building after lunch…": the user's request, then zrb's sentence word for word.

Only echo cancellation before transcription fixes that, and every platform has one: many Windows laptops' audio drivers cancel echo for every recording, Linux has PulseAudio's and PipeWire's echo-cancel modules (the WebRTC canceller browsers use) once the user loads them, and macOS has voice processing (`kAudioUnitSubType_VoiceProcessingIO`, what calls use), which each app has to ask for. On macOS, only zrb can.

**Decision.** With `enable_echo_cancel` (`ZRB_LLM_DICTATION_ENABLE_ECHO_CANCEL`, on under `ZRB_LLM_VOICE=conversation`), `listen` opens the microphone as `EchoCancelledMicrophone`: an `AVAudioEngine` whose input node is in voice-processing mode, tapped, resampled to the transcriber's rate and cut into the same blocks the sounddevice stream delivers. It stands in for that stream (`active`, `close`, the same callback), so the utterance cutter, the guards and push-to-talk are untouched.

- **Capture only.** Voice processing on macOS cancels what the output device plays, not only what its own engine plays: a clip played through a separate sounddevice stream was taken out of the microphone (its loudness envelope correlated 0.66–0.83 with the plain microphone, 0.01–0.15 through voice processing; vosk transcribed it nearly whole from one and not at all from the other), and the user's voice over it stayed intact by ear. So zrb's speech keeps playing in process (ADR-0103), and pausing it while the user talks keeps working.
- **Least ducking.** Voice processing turns other apps' audio down by default. zrb asks for the minimum, which cancelled as well in the same measurement and leaves the user's music and calls alone.
- **Never at the cost of the microphone.** Off macOS, with `ZRB_LLM_DICTATION_DEVICE` set (voice processing uses the system default microphone), or without `pyobjc-framework-AVFoundation`, the plain microphone opens and zrb logs why once. When voice processing will not start, or starts and hears nothing within two seconds (macOS can stop it right after starting with a Bluetooth headset as the default device, and the engine then never delivers a block), it is released (tap off, engine stopped, voice processing off, so it holds nothing the plain microphone needs) and the plain microphone opens with a warning.
- **Linux is configuration.** The echo-cancel module is the system's to load; zrb records from the default microphone and plays to the default speakers, so making the module's source and sink the defaults covers both. The voice guide says how.

**Consequences.** On a Mac's speakers the user can talk over zrb and be transcribed alone, with no tuning and no warm-up setting. The guards stay, because what they keep out (Whisper writing words for faint audio, one-word leftovers) does not come from echo; with zrb's voice taken out, the bar over zrb measures little of it and sits near `threshold`, as ADR-0105 expects. Voice processing also evens out the user's level, which raises distant voices in the room: `noise_margin` covers a room that talks. For the first second or two of zrb speaking the canceller is still adapting, and a burst can cross the bar; the guards handle it as any noise. While zrb listens through voice processing, another app recording the plain microphone hears it very faintly (measured: room level from 0.002 to 0.0003), as with any app on a call; a recording app that needs the microphone meanwhile wants push-to-talk, or echo cancellation off. The `voice` extra gains `pyobjc-framework-AVFoundation` on macOS. The engine's quirks are in the code: its output side must exist before voice processing is switched on, or it fails to start (-10875), and it delivers nine identical channels.

**Alternatives rejected.**

| Alternative | Why rejected |
|---|---|
| zrb's own echo canceller | Built and removed (ADR-0105): 600 lines and eighteen settings, and on laptop speakers it left a residue near the threshold. The system's canceller is the same kind of filter, tuned by the platform, at no cost to zrb. |
| Playing zrb's speech through the same `AVAudioEngine` | Not needed: voice processing cancels the separate sounddevice stream. It would have moved pausing, resuming and the player fallbacks into macOS-only code. |
| Resampling in the engine (a mixer node at 16 kHz) | The engine started, but the microphone delivered silence; a NumPy resampler on the tapped buffers is portable and testable on Linux CI. |
| Loading the Linux echo-cancel module from zrb | It changes the system's default devices for every app; that is the user's call, and a documented `pactl` line or PipeWire file makes it once. |
| On by default everywhere | Voice processing reshapes the user's level and needs a pyobjc dependency; it is on where barge-in is (`conversation`), and opt-in otherwise. |

**Where it lives.** `src/zrb/llm/dictation/echo_cancel.py` (`EchoCancelledMicrophone`, `Resampler`, `Rechunker`, `get_unavailable_reason`), `src/zrb/llm/dictation/listen.py` (`_open_microphone` choosing it), `src/zrb/config/mixins/llm_dictation.py` and `llm_voice.py` (the setting and the preset), `docs/llm/voice-camera.md` (Hearing zrb in what you say, Linux included).
