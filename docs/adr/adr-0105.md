# ADR-0105 — Barge-in guards against zrb's own voice instead of cancelling it

**Status:** Accepted

**Context.** Barge-in (ADR-0076) lets the user talk over zrb. On a laptop's own speakers the microphone hears zrb too, and without care zrb transcribes its own reply and answers it: "Goodnight. Sleep well." came back as the turns "sleep" and "well", and noise near the threshold came back from Whisper as "and this and this", each answered as if the user had said it.

An in-process echo canceller (a NumPy partitioned-block adaptive filter, delay measured by GCC-PHAT, the microphone stamped with the host's ADC times, a readiness check before the microphone was trusted over zrb) was built first. Measured on a MacBook's speakers and microphone, it worked once it had learned the room: no block of zrb's voice was left above the 0.01 threshold. But room noise sat at 44 % of that threshold and the canceller's residue at 86 %, so a slightly louder sentence still started an utterance, and Whisper, given faint clean speech, transcribed zrb's words out of it. Every phantom turn in the real sessions came after cancellation was ready.

It cost about 600 lines, playback-timing plumbing and eighteen tuning settings, for the part the cheap guards below handle anyway.

Other voice agents report the same: with macOS Voice Isolation, NousResearch's hermes-agent still trips on its own playback, and its desktop listener has no echo cancellation, relying on a threshold multiplier and transcript guards; Pipecat and LiveKit ship minimum-word and false-interruption handling for the same reason.

**Decision.** zrb does not subtract its own voice from the microphone. With `barge_in_enabled`, speech heard over zrb holds its voice at once, before anything about it is known, and the words then decide whether it stays held. What is heard reaches the model only through three guards, cheapest first. The second applies to everything heard hands-free; push-to-talk keeps every word, since its transcript goes to the input box for the user to edit.

1. **A bar over zrb's voice.** While zrb speaks, a block is loud only at `barge_in_margin` times the median level the microphone heard over zrb's last three seconds, never below `threshold`. The bar follows volume and room with nothing to configure; on headphones the microphone hears no zrb and the bar is `threshold`. Until half a second of zrb is measured, the bar is `barge_in_margin` x `threshold`. An utterance keeps the bar it started with, so the user's own voice, measured while they talk over zrb, never cuts them off; once it is a barge-in, zrb is paused and its blocks are not measured. This alone keeps zrb's voice from starting utterances, the root of every phantom turn.
2. **Not the transcriber guessing.** A Whisper model's segments are dropped when Whisper's own scores say they are not speech: no-speech probability over 0.6 with average log-probability under -1, or a compression ratio over 2.4 (text repeating itself), the Whisper project's thresholds. Any backend's transcript is dropped when it is a phrase Whisper-like models write for silence ("Thank you for watching.") or one phrase repeated ("and this and this", "the top of the top of the top"). A stop word or a yes/no is never dropped this way ("no no" is meant), nor is anything starting with a wake word: "hey zed stop, hey zed stop" is someone repeating a stop that went unheard, not a transcriber inventing words.
3. **At least two words to interrupt.** Fewer than `barge_in_min_words` (2) words said over zrb, or while a turn runs, is taken for zrb's voice or noise ("sleep", "well"), unless it is a stop word or an answer to the prompt being asked. "wait" and "hold on" are stop words.

Two settings sharpen what is heard while zrb is **silent**, where a room's own conversation gets in rather than zrb's voice. `noise_margin` raises the bar to that multiple of the quietest the room was heard at over the last few seconds (the same measure-what-you-compete-with trick), so a room that never falls quiet is measured at its own level and the bar sits above it. `min_words` is guard 3's counterpart for an utterance that interrupts nothing, so a public place can be made to need more than one stray word.

With the `vosk` backend, guard 2 also covers vosk's per-word confidence (`vosk_confidence`, 0: off): hands-free drops a transcript whose words were heard too faintly on average; push-to-talk keeps every word. None of these tells a stranger's request from the user's; only a wake word does.

**Stopping is a separate question from being heard.** Being talked over is worth answering before anything is known about what was said, so the hold is taken on loudness; whether the hold becomes a stop, cancels the turn, or is given back is answered from the words (`interrupt_judge`). A stop word said alone (`stop_words`, `polite_words`) is exact, free and final. Anything else that interrupts is put to a small model (`interrupt_judge_model`, else `llm_small_model`), which answers with a typed verdict, `stop` or `ask`, rather than a phrase to re-parse: an expletive in the middle ("please fucking stop"), the same stop said twice, a stop in another language, or a question ("can you shut up?"). The word lists decide first and stand when the model is slow, unconfigured, unsure or fails, and the model is never asked while an approval is pending, where "no" denies a tool call rather than the turn. `interrupt_judge_enabled=off` leaves the word lists deciding alone.

Anything a guard drops leaves zrb carrying on where it paused, with a badge saying why (`🎤 ignored "…" (the transcriber guessing at noise)`).

**Known cost, kept deliberately.** A hold taken on loudness alone is sometimes wrong: zrb's own voice crosses the bar too, and the hold is then given back with `resume_speech`, heard as a short stutter. That is the price of going quiet before the words are known, paid only while zrb speaks over a microphone that hears it. Holding nothing until the words arrive was tried and is worse: a transcriber that finishes a sentence at a time delivers the words after they were said, so zrb talks over the whole interruption. `barge_in_margin` and `barge_in_min_speech` keep wrong holds rare.

**The cost can be declined.** `LLM_DICTATION_BARGE_IN_HOLD` (on by default) decides whether that hold is taken at all. Off, nothing is held on loudness: zrb speaks on until the words are known to be meant for it, so a room loud enough to keep crossing the bar cannot make it stutter, the case the margin and minimum speech cannot keep out alone. Barge-in itself is untouched: what stops zrb is the words. A stop word or wake word in the live transcript of a backend that transcribes while you speak is acted on as soon as heard; a batch backend leaves the stop to the finished utterance. That delay is what the setting trades for quiet, which is why the hold stays on by default.

The room is measured from everything heard while zrb is silent, the user's own speech included (nothing in the audio tells the two apart, hence the wake word for public places). So in a quiet room a long uninterrupted sentence becomes the floor itself, and the bar sits above the user's next words until a block about a quarter as loud as their voice (a breath or sentence break) enters the three-second window. Keeping a room that never falls quiet out was chosen over never holding the user back; `noise_margin=0` trades it back.

**Consequences.** Barge-in works on speakers with no warm-up and no native library, at the cost of the user speaking up a little over laptop speakers, a lone word over zrb (other than a stop word or an answer) being ignored, and a brief pause-and-resume when the microphone's pickup of zrb crosses the bar. The same cost returns in a noisy room, where a soft voice that cleared a fixed threshold must now clear the room; `noise_margin=0` restores the fixed threshold. Each guard is imperfect: the word match misses heavily misheard echo, and loud echo can be transcribed as other words, which is why the loudness bar comes first. An interrupting utterance costs one small-model call unless a stop word settles it, so the voice path wants a fast local model; a slow or unavailable judge leaves the word lists deciding. A user who wants no risk keeps barge-in off: zrb and the user take turns, as `zrb-extras`' listen and speak tools always did. A user who wants barge-in without the stutter keeps the hold off and gives up reacting before the words. In-process playback (ADR-0103) stays, since pausing speech while the user talks needs it.

**Alternatives rejected.**

| Alternative | Why rejected |
| --- | --- |
| zrb's own echo canceller (NumPy MDF filter, measured delay, readiness check) | Built and measured: it removed zrb's echo down to the room noise yet left every phantom turn, which came from the margin above the threshold and from Whisper, neither of which it touches. 600 lines, eighteen settings. |
| WebRTC's canceller (livekit) | No wheel for Termux, musl Linux or Windows on ARM, so it could not ship in `zrb[voice]`; and better cancellation only moves the same margin. |
| The operating system's voice processing (macOS Voice Isolation, PipeWire echo-cancel) | Per platform, needs capture through the OS audio framework, and still trips on a MacBook's own playback in another agent. A user who has it can rely on it: the bar then stays near `threshold`. |
| Holding nothing until the words arrive (a wake word deciding the hold) | Tried (with wake words set, loudness held nothing and the transcript took the hold). A transcriber that finishes a sentence at a time (`gpt-4o-transcribe`, any batch backend) delivers the words after the user stops talking, so zrb talked through the whole interruption: "I keep saying stop and it keeps talking". LiveKit's `resume_false_interruption` and Pipecat's VAD turn-start strategies duck first and hand the hold back when the words are not a stop, the shape zrb uses. |
| Letting the model decide everything, with no word lists | A stop word is exact, free, and works with no model, no key and no network; the judge is the fallback for what a word list cannot express, not the first resort. |
| Telling zrb from the user by the words heard alone | Loud echo is transcribed as other words ("doctor", "jeffrey"), so the words alone do not tell the two apart. |
| A fixed bar over zrb (a threshold multiplier) | Too strict quietly, too loose loudly; measuring zrb's level keeps one margin right at every volume. |
| Speaker verification (recognising the user's voice) | Needs a voice model and an enrolment; far heavier than the problem. |

**Where it lives.** `src/zrb/llm/dictation/listen.py` (`UtteranceCutter`: the bar over zrb's voice, and the bar under ordinary speech), `src/zrb/llm/dictation/feature.py` (`DictationSession`: the hold, the transcript guards, and what the words are taken to ask), `src/zrb/llm/dictation/interrupt_judge.py` and `src/zrb/llm/prompt/markdown/barge_in.md` (the verdict, and the prompt a project may override), `src/zrb/llm/dictation/words.py` (`is_transcriber_guess`, `count_words`), `src/zrb/llm/dictation/backend/openai.py` (Whisper's segment scores), `src/zrb/llm/dictation/backend/vosk.py` (vosk's per-word confidence), `src/zrb/config/mixins/llm_dictation.py`.
