# ADR-0105 — Barge-in guards against zrb's own voice instead of cancelling it

**Status:** Accepted

**Context.** Barge-in (ADR-0076) lets the user talk over zrb. On a laptop's own speakers the microphone hears zrb too, and without care zrb transcribes its own reply and answers it: "Goodnight. Sleep well." came back as the turns "sleep" and "well", and noise near the threshold came back from Whisper as "and this and this", each answered by the model as if the user had said it.

An in-process echo canceller (a NumPy partitioned-block adaptive filter, with the delay measured by GCC-PHAT, the microphone stamped with the host's ADC times, and a readiness check before the microphone was trusted over zrb) was built first. Measured on a MacBook's speakers and microphone, it worked once it had learned the room: no block of zrb's voice was left above the 0.01 threshold. But the room noise sat at 44 % of that threshold and what the canceller left at 86 % of it, so a slightly louder sentence still started an utterance, and Whisper, given faint clean speech, transcribed zrb's words out of it. Every phantom turn in the real sessions came after cancellation was ready.

The canceller cost about 600 lines, its playback-timing plumbing, and eighteen tuning settings, for the part of the problem the cheap guards below handle anyway. Other voice agents report the same: with macOS's own Voice Isolation on a MacBook, NousResearch's hermes-agent still trips on its own playback, and its desktop listener has no echo cancellation at all, relying on a threshold multiplier and transcript guards; Pipecat and LiveKit ship minimum-word and false-interruption handling for the same reason.

**Decision.** zrb does not subtract its own voice from the microphone. With `barge_in_enabled`, speech heard over zrb holds its voice at once, before anything about it is known, and the words then decide whether it stays held; what is heard reaches the model only through three guards, cheapest first. The second guard applies to everything heard hands-free; push-to-talk keeps every word, since its transcript goes to the input box for the user to edit.

1. **A bar over zrb's voice.** While zrb speaks, a block is loud only at `barge_in_margin` (3, about 10 dB) times the median level the microphone heard over zrb's last three seconds, never below `threshold`. The bar follows the volume and the room with nothing to configure; on headphones the microphone hears no zrb and the bar is `threshold`. Until half a second of zrb is measured, the bar is `barge_in_margin` × `threshold`. An utterance keeps the bar it started with, so the user's own voice, measured while they talk over zrb, never cuts them off; once it is a barge-in, zrb is paused and its blocks are not measured at all. This alone keeps zrb's voice from starting utterances, the root of every phantom turn.
2. **Not the transcriber guessing.** A Whisper model's segments are dropped when Whisper's own scores say they are not speech: no-speech probability over 0.6 with average log-probability under −1, or a compression ratio over 2.4 (text repeating itself), the thresholds the Whisper project uses. Any backend's transcript is dropped when it is a phrase Whisper-like models write for silence ("Thank you for watching.") or one phrase over and over ("and this and this", "the top of the top of the top"). A stop word or a yes/no is never dropped this way ("no no" is meant), and neither is anything starting with a wake word: a repeat of a phrase with a wake word at the front ("hey zed stop, hey zed stop") is someone saying stop a second time, not a transcriber inventing words, and it is exactly what someone does when the first one went unheard.
3. **At least two words to interrupt.** Fewer than `barge_in_min_words` (2) words said over zrb, or while a turn runs, is taken for zrb's voice or noise ("sleep", "well"), unless it is a stop word or an answer to the prompt being asked. "wait" and "hold on" are stop words.

**The transcript-level echo guards are retired.** This ADR used to carry a third guard for zrb's own words: the speaker logged each sentence it played and when (`spoken_log`), and a transcript heard over zrb's voice that was mostly words it was saying then was dropped. `self_echo_match`, `self_echo_tail` and `echo_cooldown` are gone with it. It was a patch for an audio-level problem at the transcript level, it only ever caught the echo that had already cleared the loudness bar, and what it caught is what guard 3 and the judge answer anyway — so the cost of removing it is real but bounded: zrb's own last word can now open a turn where it was once dropped, and `self_echo_match=0` was already the way to switch it off. Replacing it properly is not in zrb yet: either an echo canceller, which this ADR rejected once and would reject again on the same grounds, or a turn model that reads the words rather than matching them.

Two settings sharpen what is heard while zrb is **silent**, which is where a room's own conversation gets in rather than zrb's own voice. `noise_margin` (2) raises the bar to that multiple of the quietest the room was heard at over the last few seconds — the same measure-the-thing-you-compete-with trick as the bar over zrb's voice, so a room that never falls quiet is measured at its own level and the bar sits above it. `min_words` (1) is guard 4's counterpart for an utterance that interrupts nothing, so a public place can be made to need more than one stray word.

With the `vosk` backend, guard 2 also covers vosk's own per-word confidence (`vosk_confidence`, 0: off), which zrb discarded before: hands-free drops a transcript whose words were heard too faintly on average, while push-to-talk keeps every word. None of these tells a stranger's request from the user's — only a wake word does.

**Stopping is a separate question from being heard.** Being talked over is worth answering before anything is known about what was said, so the hold is taken on loudness; whether the hold becomes a stop, cancels the turn, or is given back is answered from the words (`interrupt_judge`). A stop word said alone (`stop_words`, `polite_words`) is exact, free and final. Anything else that interrupts is put to a small model (`interrupt_judge_model`, else `llm_small_model`), which answers with a typed verdict — `stop` or `ask` — rather than a phrase zrb has to re-parse: an expletive in the middle ("please fucking stop"), the same stop said twice, a stop in another language, or a question ("can you shut up?"). The word lists decide first and stand when the model is slow, unconfigured or unsure, and the model is never asked while an approval is pending, where "no" denies a tool call rather than the turn. `interrupt_judge_enabled=off` leaves the word lists deciding alone.

Anything a guard drops leaves zrb carrying on where it paused, with a badge saying why (`🎤 ignored "…" (Zrb's own voice)`, named by `ZRB_LLM_ASSISTANT_NAME`).

**Known cost, kept deliberately.** A hold taken on loudness alone is sometimes wrong: zrb's own voice crosses the bar too, and the hold is then given back with `resume_speech`, heard as a short stutter of a sentence. That is the price of going quiet before the words are known, and it is paid only while zrb is speaking over a microphone that hears it; the alternative — holding nothing until the words arrive — was tried and is worse, because with a transcriber that finishes a sentence at a time the words arrive after it has been said, so zrb talks over the whole interruption. `barge_in_margin` and `barge_in_min_speech` are what keep the wrong holds rare.

The room is measured from everything heard while zrb is silent, the user's own speech included — nothing in the audio tells the two apart, which is why this ADR falls back on a wake word for public places. So in a quiet room a long uninterrupted sentence becomes the floor itself, and the bar sits above the user's next words until a block about a quarter as loud as their voice — a breath or a sentence break — enters the three-second window. Keeping a room that never falls quiet out was chosen over never holding the user back; `noise_margin=0` trades it back.

**Consequences.** Barge-in works on speakers with no warm-up and no native library, at the cost of the user speaking up a little over laptop speakers, a lone word over zrb (other than a stop word or an answer) being ignored, and a brief pause-and-resume when the microphone's own pickup of zrb crosses the bar. The same cost comes back in a noisy room, where a soft voice that cleared a fixed threshold must now clear the room; `noise_margin=0` puts the fixed threshold back. The guards are each imperfect: the word match misses heavily misheard echo, and loud echo can be transcribed as other words, which is why the loudness bar comes first. An interrupting utterance now costs one small-model call unless a stop word settles it, so the voice path wants a fast local model; a judge that is slow or unavailable leaves the word lists deciding, which is what zrb did before there was one. A user who wants no risk at all keeps barge-in off: zrb and the user take turns, as `zrb-extras`' listen and speak tools always did. In-process playback (ADR-0103) stays, since pausing speech while the user talks needs it.

**Alternatives rejected.**

| Alternative | Why rejected |
| --- | --- |
| zrb's own echo canceller (NumPy MDF filter, measured delay, readiness check) | Built and measured: it removed zrb's echo down to the room noise, yet left every phantom turn in place, since those came from the margin above the threshold and from Whisper, which it does not touch. 600 lines and eighteen settings. |
| WebRTC's canceller (livekit) | No wheel for Termux, musl Linux or Windows on ARM, so it could not ship in `zrb[voice]`; and better cancellation only moves the same margin. |
| The operating system's voice processing (macOS Voice Isolation, PipeWire echo-cancel) | Per platform, needs capture through the OS audio framework, and still trips on a MacBook's own playback in another agent. A user who has it can rely on it: the bar then stays near `threshold`. |
| Holding nothing until the words arrive (a wake word deciding the hold) | Tried, and it is what this ADR used to decide: with wake words set, loudness held nothing and the transcript took the hold. With a transcriber that finishes a sentence at a time — `gpt-4o-transcribe`, or any batch backend, which is most of them — the words arrive after the user has stopped talking, so zrb talked through the whole interruption: the reported "I keep saying stop and it keeps talking". The frameworks that face this (LiveKit's `resume_false_interruption`, Pipecat's VAD turn-start strategies) duck first and hand the hold back when the words turn out not to be a stop, which is the shape zrb uses. |
| Letting the model decide everything, with no word lists | A stop word is exact, free, and works with no model, no key and no network; the judge is the fallback for what a word list cannot express, not the first resort. |
| Telling zrb from the user by the words heard alone | Loud echo is transcribed as other words ("doctor", "jeffrey"); kept only as the third guard, behind the loudness bar. |
| A fixed bar over zrb (a threshold multiplier) | Too strict quietly, too loose loudly; measuring zrb's level keeps one margin right at every volume. |
| Speaker verification (recognising the user's voice) | Needs a voice model and an enrolment; far heavier than the problem. |

**Where it lives.** `src/zrb/llm/dictation/listen.py` (`UtteranceCutter`: the bar over zrb's voice, and the bar under ordinary speech), `src/zrb/llm/dictation/feature.py` (`DictationSession`: the hold, the transcript guards, and what the words are taken to ask), `src/zrb/llm/dictation/interrupt_judge.py` and `src/zrb/llm/prompt/markdown/barge_in.md` (the verdict, and the prompt a project may override), `src/zrb/llm/dictation/words.py` (`is_transcriber_guess`, `count_words`), `src/zrb/llm/dictation/backend/openai.py` (Whisper's segment scores), `src/zrb/llm/dictation/backend/vosk.py` (vosk's per-word confidence), `src/zrb/config/mixins/llm_dictation.py`.
