# ADR-0105 — Barge-in guards against zrb's own voice instead of cancelling it

**Status:** Accepted

**Context.** Barge-in (ADR-0076) lets the user talk over zrb. On a laptop's own speakers the microphone hears zrb too, and without care zrb transcribes its own reply and answers it: "Goodnight. Sleep well." came back as the turns "sleep" and "well", and noise near the threshold came back from Whisper as "and this and this", each answered by the model as if the user had said it.

An in-process echo canceller (a NumPy partitioned-block adaptive filter, with the delay measured by GCC-PHAT, the microphone stamped with the host's ADC times, and a readiness check before the microphone was trusted over zrb) was built first. Measured on a MacBook's speakers and microphone, it worked once it had learned the room: no block of zrb's voice was left above the 0.01 threshold. But the room noise sat at 44 % of that threshold and what the canceller left at 86 % of it, so a slightly louder sentence still started an utterance, and Whisper, given faint clean speech, transcribed zrb's words out of it. Every phantom turn in the real sessions came after cancellation was ready.

The canceller cost about 600 lines, its playback-timing plumbing, and eighteen tuning settings, for the part of the problem the cheap guards below handle anyway. Other voice agents report the same: with macOS's own Voice Isolation on a MacBook, NousResearch's hermes-agent still trips on its own playback, and its desktop listener has no echo cancellation at all, relying on a threshold multiplier and transcript guards; Pipecat and LiveKit ship minimum-word and false-interruption handling for the same reason.

**Decision.** zrb does not subtract its own voice from the microphone. With `barge_in_enabled`, what is heard over zrb reaches the model only through four guards, cheapest first, and a barge-in pauses zrb before it stops it. The second guard applies to everything heard hands-free; push-to-talk keeps every word, since its transcript goes to the input box for the user to edit.

1. **A bar over zrb's voice.** While zrb speaks, and for `echo_cooldown` after, a block is loud only at `barge_in_margin` (3, about 10 dB) times the median level the microphone heard over zrb's last three seconds, never below `threshold`. The bar follows the volume and the room with nothing to configure; on headphones the microphone hears no zrb and the bar is `threshold`. Until half a second of zrb is measured, the bar is `barge_in_margin` × `threshold`. An utterance keeps the bar it started with, so the user's own voice, measured while they talk over zrb, never cuts them off; once it is a barge-in, zrb is paused and its blocks are not measured at all. This alone keeps zrb's voice from starting utterances, the root of every phantom turn.
2. **Not the transcriber guessing.** A Whisper model's segments are dropped when Whisper's own scores say they are not speech: no-speech probability over 0.6 with average log-probability under −1, or a compression ratio over 2.4 (text repeating itself), the thresholds the Whisper project uses. Any backend's transcript is dropped when it is a phrase Whisper-like models write for silence ("Thank you for watching.") or one phrase over and over ("and this and this", "the top of the top of the top"). A stop word or a yes/no is never dropped this way: "no no" is meant.
3. **Not zrb's own words.** zrb's speaker logs each sentence it plays and when (`spoken_log`), only while it is heard: from when its playback starts, and not while paused, so the user talking into zrb's silence is never taken for zrb. A transcript heard over zrb's voice, at least `self_echo_match` (0.8) of whose words zrb was saying while it was heard, or for `self_echo_tail` after, or which reads like a stretch of it letter by letter (60 %, ten letters or more), is zrb's voice and is dropped. Heard while zrb was silent, it is the user's, even in zrb's words ("run the tests", to "Should I run the tests?").
4. **At least two words to interrupt.** Fewer than `barge_in_min_words` (2) words said over zrb, or while a turn runs, is taken for zrb's voice or noise ("sleep", "well"), unless it is a stop word or an answer to the prompt being asked. "wait" and "hold on" are stop words.

Anything a guard drops leaves zrb carrying on where it paused, with a badge saying why (`🎤 ignored "…" (Zrb's own voice)`, named by `ZRB_LLM_ASSISTANT_NAME`).

**Consequences.** Barge-in works on speakers with no warm-up and no native library, at the cost of the user speaking up a little over laptop speakers and a lone word over zrb (other than a stop word or an answer) being ignored. The guards are each imperfect: the word match misses heavily misheard echo, and loud echo can be transcribed as other words, which is why the loudness bar comes first. A user who wants no risk at all keeps barge-in off: zrb and the user take turns, as `zrb-extras`' listen and speak tools always did. In-process playback (ADR-0103) stays, since pausing speech while the user talks needs it.

**Alternatives rejected.**

| Alternative | Why rejected |
| --- | --- |
| zrb's own echo canceller (NumPy MDF filter, measured delay, readiness check) | Built and measured: it removed zrb's echo down to the room noise, yet left every phantom turn in place, since those came from the margin above the threshold and from Whisper, which it does not touch. 600 lines and eighteen settings. |
| WebRTC's canceller (livekit) | No wheel for Termux, musl Linux or Windows on ARM, so it could not ship in `zrb[voice]`; and better cancellation only moves the same margin. |
| The operating system's voice processing (macOS Voice Isolation, PipeWire echo-cancel) | Per platform, needs capture through the OS audio framework, and still trips on a MacBook's own playback in another agent. A user who has it can rely on it: the bar then stays near `threshold`. |
| Telling zrb from the user by the words heard alone | Loud echo is transcribed as other words ("doctor", "jeffrey"); kept only as the third guard, behind the loudness bar. |
| A fixed bar over zrb (a threshold multiplier) | Too strict quietly, too loose loudly; measuring zrb's level keeps one margin right at every volume. |
| Speaker verification (recognising the user's voice) | Needs a voice model and an enrolment; far heavier than the problem. |

**Where it lives.** `src/zrb/llm/dictation/listen.py` (`UtteranceCutter`: the bar over zrb's voice), `src/zrb/llm/dictation/feature.py` (`DictationSession`: the transcript guards), `src/zrb/llm/dictation/words.py` (`is_transcriber_guess`, `is_said_back`, `count_words`), `src/zrb/llm/dictation/backend/openai.py` (Whisper's segment scores), `src/zrb/llm/speech/spoken_log.py`, `src/zrb/config/mixins/llm_dictation.py`.
