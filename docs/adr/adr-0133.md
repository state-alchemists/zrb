# ADR-0133 — A panel that has something to say docks into the layout, and takes no rows it cannot paint

**Status:** Accepted.

**Context.** Three surfaces answered the user inside the transcript's own screen: the choice panel (`AskUserQuestion`), the `/btw` answer, and the agent picker. All three were `Float`s over the transcript with a height that followed their content.

A float is the wrong shape for a surface the user has to read. Whatever is under it cannot be read, and while it holds the focus the transcript cannot even be scrolled — ADR-0117 deliberately gave the `/btw` panel that focus. On a 24-row terminal the float covered most of the transcript; on a 16-row one it covered all of it, and prompt_toolkit painted its "window too small" note instead of the layout. Shrinking the panel was not a fix either: the choice panel answered a shrinking screen by dropping the question's `context`, which is the part that makes the question answerable.

**Decision.**

1. **A docked panel takes its rows out of the layout, not out of what is painted.** `create_layout` places the panels in the vertical split — `dock_panels` above the input slot — instead of a `Float`. The completions menu stays the layout's only float, because it is transient and needs no focus.

2. **A panel whose input is dead may take the input frame's place; a panel that keeps the input alive docks above it.** The choice and the picker replace the frame: while one is open, typing belongs to it, and the input's own Enter would contradict the answer being chosen. `/btw` docks above the frame, because its `Keys.Any` handler ends in `insert_input_text` — a typed character there is the start of a message, and hiding the field it lands in would make that typing blind.

3. **What a panel may take is a share of the screen, not however many rows its content needs.** `panel_row_cap` is a third of the terminal (`PANEL_SCREEN_DIVISOR`), floored at `PANEL_MIN_ROWS` while the layout can spare them, and never more than `rows - LAYOUT_MIN_ROWS` — the title bar, info bar, both spacers, the input frame, the hint line, the status bar and one transcript row. The height handed to the container is that number exactly (`framed_panel_height`), so a panel neither grows with its content nor bargains with the transcript for rows.

4. **The cap is applied by painting a slice, because the surface cannot scroll.** Two of the three render through a `FormattedTextControl`, whose rows *are* the whole window. `RowWindow` keeps a fixed-height slice on the row the cursor is on — an anchor row that scrolls past either edge pulls the window with it, so a twenty-option question never hides the option under the cursor — and the last rows stay painted whatever the window does, because they are the key hint and a legend nobody can see is not a legend.

5. **No panel activates where it cannot paint a row.** Below `panel_row_cap == 0` the content goes to the transcript and the panel stays closed: the choice closes and arms its own free-text prefix so a typed answer still resolves, the picker declines to open, `/btw` and every command reply (`show`, `fail`) append to the transcript. A reply written into a buffer nobody paints is worse than no panel, because the user sees nothing and waits. The same holds for a screen that shrinks under a panel already open: hiding it is not enough, because an active choice still makes the input read-only and still lets Enter confirm the highlighted row, and an open picker still holds the keys. `fall_back_when_unpaintable` closes all three on the render that sees the new size — `after_render` is where prompt_toolkit reports a resize — which is the transition the rule is about, not the state. What only the panel held moves with it: a finished `/btw` answer is appended to the transcript under its question, and closing a question that already went to the transcript drops its request token, so an answer that lands after the user dismissed it stays out.

6. **PageUp/PageDown always walk the transcript, whichever pane holds the focus.** `page_output` names the transcript rather than reading `event.current_buffer`, and leaves `PAGE_OVERLAP_ROWS` rows of overlap to orient on. A docked panel leaves the transcript readable, and moving a choice's cursor is Up/Down's job.

**Consequences.** The transcript keeps two thirds of the screen and stays readable while a panel is up, and it is reachable by paging even while a panel holds the focus. A panel is now a fixed window over its own content rather than a view of all of it: content longer than the cap is reachable only by moving the anchor, which is why the rows that matter (the cursor's, and the key hint) are the ones kept. `LAYOUT_MIN_ROWS` couples the cap to the layout's own bars — a layout change that adds or drops a bar has to move that number with it — and every test that asserts a height has to pin `terminal_rows`, because a 24-row CI terminal is not the developer's terminal.

**Alternatives rejected.**

- *Keep the float and make it smaller* — that was the first attempt: a shrunken panel bought its rows by dropping the question's `context`, which is exactly the content that makes a question answerable.
- *Let the panel grow with its content and let the layout clip it* — clipping cuts the key hint and the row under the cursor first, which are the two rows the user needs.
- *Give the panels a `Buffer` so they scroll natively* — a `FormattedTextControl` returns rows, not a document; converting all three surfaces to buffer-driven rendering is a rewrite of each one's renderer, for a scrollbar the slice already provides.
- *Refuse to open a panel below the threshold and say nothing* — a question that does not appear cannot be answered, and a command reply that does not appear is a command that did nothing.

**Where it lives.** `src/zrb/llm/ui/default/app/docking.py` (`panel_row_cap`, `framed_panel_height`, `RowWindow`, `LAYOUT_MIN_ROWS`), `app/layout.py` (`dock_panels`, `input_panel`, `_input_slot`), `app/keybinding.py` (`page_output`), `selection.py` (the choice in the input's slot, and the unpaintable fallbacks), `agent_picker.py`, `side_question.py`. See ADR-0117 for the `/btw` focus this record keeps, and ADR-0106 for the side question it answers.
