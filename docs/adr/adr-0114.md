# ADR-0114 — Listening follows a change in the audio devices, and speech prebuffers against its own stutter

**Status:** Accepted

**Context.** Barge-in (ADR-0076) is hands-free listening with speech playing in process (ADR-0103): a `sounddevice` input stream stays open the whole session, and every sentence opens an output stream of its own. PortAudio has no portable callback for hardware changing — a headset plugged in or taken off, a monitor's speakers appearing, the system default moved elsewhere. Nothing detected any of it:

- The microphone stream was only re-opened when it *died* (the loop's `stream.active` check, where macOS stops voice processing on a route change, ADR-0111). A stream that survived the change — PulseAudio migrates it, and some CoreAudio paths keep it alive while shifting the device — carried hands-free listening on with the old hardware, in silence or on a microphone nobody wears any more.
- Playback had no signal at all: an output stream losing its device mid-sentence just blocks the playing thread until `_finished` is answered by some later `stop`, and the audio that ran short (a download slower than playback, a callback too late) rendered as silence nobody could tell from intent.

Both directions replay PortAudio's `status` flag, which has been discarded everywhere, even though it is exactly the report of samples gained (input overflow) or missed (output underflow).

**Decision.**

- *Device watch.* `DeviceWatcher` (`src/zrb/llm/util/audio_devices.py`) snapshots `sd.query_devices()` plus the *resolved* defaults — `query_devices(kind="input")` and `kind="output"`, what PortAudio opens without being asked for one. Devices are read as index, name and channel counts, which make one out when plugged, unplugged or renamed. The resolved defaults are read instead of `sd.default.device`, which carries at most what somebody set by hand (and on the shipped `sounddevice` answers with the live resolved pair, but only through `query_devices` is the form a reopen follows pinned): an operating system default moved to a device that was there all along changes the pair while the device list says the same — the sentinel pair `sd.default.device` used to answer (`(-1, -1)`) could never say that. `has_changed` re-reads at most once per *poll_seconds*, answering whether the view moved since the last ask. A read that raises (during a device transition PortAudio can fail for a moment to enumerate the hardware, or name the default) is no snapshot: the watcher keeps the last readable one instead of ending the listening, whether the failure is a structural one, a `RuntimeError`/`OSError`, or the `sounddevice.PortAudioError` of the `sd` at hand. `listen` watches only while PortAudio opens its own default (a microphone named in `LLM_DICTATION_DEVICE` is not moved for a speaker being plugged in, and the view is not even read) and not for the echo-cancelled microphone, whose relation to a route change is its own (macOS stops voice processing on it, where `listen` already reopens). On a change the microphone stream is closed and opened again, with a gap marked, so the utterance is never spliced across the gap.
- *Dropped audio is said, and said off the callback.* `status.input_overflow` is reported ("the microphone dropped audio it could not capture in time") at most once per `_OVERFLOW_REPORT_SECONDS` — twice or thirty times say nothing more than once does. The audio callback only timestamps a drop: logging can block, and a delayed callback loses the blocks that follow, so the warning itself runs on the event loop, where the listening waits for the next block.
- *Playback prebuffers.* An output stream starts only once a moment of audio (`_PREBUFFER_SECONDS`, 0.1 s) is read ahead, or the source is done, or the wait deadline (`_PREBUFFER_WAIT_SECONDS`, 1.0 s) passes — what arrived goes, because a sentence that never starts is worse than one that stutters in its first block. The reader thread waits on a condition instead of polling every 5 ms, and `stop` and an abandoned playback wake it, so nothing waits on a not-notified flag.
- *Underruns are counted, and said off the callback.* `_fill` counts a block the device said it got too early (`status.output_underflow`) or that arrives shorter than asked while the source is still coming, as `underruns` — what the listener hears as stutter. The saying of it runs on the playing thread, which watches while the audio plays: logging itself can block, and a blocked callback underruns again.
- *A lost device ends the sentence, not the patience.* `_is_finished_in_time` polls `stream.active` while it waits, and looks once more when the wait itself answers: PortAudio's finished callback sets `_finished` as well, so a device it stopped playing on its own — before or with the wait ending — is read as what it is, a cut-off logged with the device named, its source closed (`_is_abandoned`, which ends the read still running behind the gone device), and the next sentence opens on whatever is the device then. A stop of our own (`is_stopped`) and a device still playing (the source played to its end) are the ends that were played to.

**Consequences.** Plugging a headset in mid-conversation moves hands-free dictation to its microphone within one device-watch poll (~2 s), and speech lands on it from the next sentence; no restart of the session is needed. The cost of the first sentence after a device change is one reopen; the cost of the watch is a `query_devices()` read at most every `DEVICE_WATCH_SECONDS` (2.0 s) while the microphone is idle. Underruns make stutter observable for the first time: `underruns` says whether "speech was cut off" was the network or the machine. The prebuffer holds the first block back by at most the deadline, so a fast download adds nothing and a slow one trades up to a second of silence for a clean start. A session that keeps playing through a device's return still pays nothing extra: each sentence reopens the device, which is where the watch pays its other way.

**Alternatives rejected.**

| Alternative | Why rejected |
|---|---|
| Platform hotplug listeners (CoreAudio property listeners, PipeWire events, WM_DEVICECHANGE) | The exact, immediate signals — each platform its own, none reachable through PortAudio's stable API. The snapshot covers all of them at once, is portable, and ~2 s of follow-up lag is what a conversation tolerates; a macOS CoreAudio listener would still owe every other platform its own mechanism. |
| One output stream kept open for the whole session | Removes every per-sentence open/close (and the stop-timeout machinery's exposure), but pauses, timeouts, device recovery and the player-program fallback all live around a stream-per-utterance; reopening on change is how each sentence follows the device now. |
| Growing `read_ahead` instead of prebuffering | The read ahead (≈2.7 s) already covers a trickle; the click is the *first block* being asked for before the source has said anything, which no running total fixes once playback has started. |
| Treating a lost device as a device error | `_handle_device_error` marks the device unavailable for the whole session, handing every later sentence to a player program; a headset that comes back would never be picked up in-process again. |

**Where it lives.** `src/zrb/llm/util/audio_devices.py` (`DeviceWatcher`, `read_device_snapshot`), `src/zrb/llm/dictation/listen.py` (the device watch in the idle branch, the overflow report, `DEVICE_WATCH_SECONDS`), `src/zrb/llm/speech/pcm_player.py` (`_prebuffer`, `underruns`, `_is_finished_in_time`'s lost-device path), `test/llm/util/test_audio_devices.py`, `test/llm/dictation/test_listen_device_watch.py`, `test/llm/speech/test_pcm_player_underrun.py`.
