## 2.53.2a1 (August 3, 2026)

- **Fix: the anti-rumination rule's escape hatch pointed back into tool-calling** (`src/zrb/llm/prompt/markdown/workflow.md`, `src/zrb/llm/tool/ask.py`, `test/llm/tool/test_ask.py`): the uncertainty ladder's stall-breaker read *"On a third pass over the same evidence, go back to step 1 or take the exit the ladder gives you"* — and step 1 is *make a tool call*. The one rule written to end a deliberation loop routed straight back into it, producing an alternation of tool call and re-think that nothing else bounded. Step 1 is self-graded (*"a tool call **can** settle it"*) and another `Grep` always looks like it might, while `Recovery` covers a repeating **error**, never a call that succeeds without narrowing the question. The rule now names that case as evidence-free and closes step 1 on the third pass, leaving step 2 (act on the best-supported option, assumption named) or step 3 (ask). Investigation-first is untouched: the counter resets on any call that does narrow the question, so a genuine multi-file investigation never trips it. No step 0 for user-intent ambiguity was added — `AskUserQuestion`'s `when_to_use` in `common_tools.py` already states that condition, and `tool_guidance` composes *after* `workflow`, so by `mandate.md`'s own precedence rule it already wins in its own scope; a second copy would be the cross-section duplication `AGENTS.md` forbids. Both `[SYSTEM SUGGESTION]` short-circuits in `ask_user_question` — non-interactive mode and no-UI — offered only *"make your best judgement and continue"*, which hands an unanswerable question straight back to the deliberation that raised it. They now offer two terminal exits, decide-and-continue with the assumption named or stop-and-report the choice that could not be made, and forbid a retry, so an unavailable question cannot become a re-ask loop.
